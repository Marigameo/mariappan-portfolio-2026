# The LinkedIn post

Keep it minimal and personal. The post doesn't explain the project. The reel teases it, and the story and blog in the comments do the explaining. The post's only job is to give a human reason to watch the reel and open the comments.

## The workflow: the user writes, Claude polishes

Posts that read as AI-written get poor engagement, so **the user writes the post**. Claude's job is to help it land, not to write it.

1. **Pointers first, then ask for a draft.** Read the story (and the blog, if there is one), then share 4 to 6 short pointers on what makes it attractive to a LinkedIn reader. Good sources are an honest or surprising moment (a quote like "It looks like AI slop"), the one idea in a sentence, something people can see in the reel, and what's relatable about the problem. Say where each one comes from in the story. Then ask the user for their own draft, rough is fine. **Don't write a draft yourself**, not even "to get started".
2. **Polish their draft.** Keep their structure, their hook and most of their words. Fix the flow, cut filler, tighten the phrasing, and check it against the benchmarks below and the anatomy and self-check further down. It should read as human, humble and subtle: no hype, no brag, no polished-marketing rhythm, and no tidy triplets or "it's not X, it's Y" lines, since those read as AI. If a line is unfinished or unclear, offer several ways to finish it and leave the lines around it alone.
3. **Hand back.** If the change is clear, give one polished version and a short note on what changed and why. If it's open (a different hook, how much to say), give **2 or 3 options** labelled A, B, C, each a small step from the draft rather than a rewrite. The user picks one and posts it.

### What the benchmarks show

- **They open on a small, real moment.** "Spent the weekend exploring…", "I was fixing a small UI bug & was not sure about the exact fix", "Had so much fun updating my portfolio this weekend". They describe what happened, not a claim about the work.
- **They play the ambition down.** "shadcn already has one. The goal wasn’t to build a better version…", "one detail at a time", "(my quick is ~5 mins) 🥲", "just a post-party shitpost". The humility is the hook.
- **They're casual.** Phrases like "&", "gonna", "pretty damn well", "agent friends 🤖" and "Crazy ride." are part of the voice. **Don't formalise them.** Fix only typos and lines that could be misread. Leave the rest as written, even where the grammar is loose ("So stepped back a little…").
- **Emoji go wider than the launch set**: 🤖 🥲 ✨ ☕️ ♥️ 🎬 🫣 😈 🎨 😮‍💨 😂 ✌️ 🔗 🛑, mostly at line ends and sometimes after two spaces. Keep the user's own emoji.
- **Unicode sans-serif bold marks a name or a call to action**, even in the middle of a line: 𝗔𝗺𝗯𝗶𝗴𝘂𝗶𝘁𝘆 𝗥𝗲𝘀𝗼𝗹𝘃𝗲𝗿, 𝗚𝗼 𝗯𝗿𝗲𝗮𝗸 𝗶𝘁. Use it for one or two short phrases at most.
- **Length depends on the kind of post.** Launches run about 60 to 100 words. A reflection like the portfolio post runs longer, in one- or two-line paragraphs with a question or an aside between them.
- **The endings are playful.** "𝗚𝗼 𝗯𝗿𝗲𝗮𝗸 𝗶𝘁. Links in the comments ✨", "Maybe this is what I get paid for? 👀", "hard stop for this week… ✌️". The pointer to the comments is always there.
- **Hashtags are optional.** None of the three newer posts uses them.

**When polishing, change as little as possible.** Polishing that's too smooth is exactly what makes a post read as AI.

## Anatomy

The body runs **60 to 90 words**, in four or five short blocks with a blank line between them:

1. **A personal hook (one or two lines).** An honest, slightly self-deprecating admission, not a claim. "This one sat in my drafts for months." / "Another story close to my heart, sitting in my drafts for months. 😅" Don't reuse the drafts line a third time. Find this project's own honest feeling, such as how long it took, what surprised you, or why you kept coming back to it.
2. **Where it sits (one or two lines).** For the first post in a series, give when it started and who "we" were: "It started in September 2025… We were a small team, none of us from AI…" For a follow-up, link back: "Last week, I shared how … evolved. This is the next evolution of that system."
3. **The idea in one sentence.** Lift the blog's one-liner or the origin quote, credited by role: "Strive Studio began with a slightly wild idea from our CTO: what if the agent just wrote the HTML?" If there's no quote, say plainly what the write-up covers: "how it started, why we built it from scratch, what broke…"
4. **Why it matters to me, with credit to the team (one line).** "It’s the kind of work that excites me most as a design engineer, and one that deserves some spotlight on the team behind it. ❤️"
5. **The pointer.** The teaser line plus "in the comments 👇". Either use plain text ("A small teaser to chew on 👀 / The detailed story is in the comments 👇"), or set this one line in Unicode sans-serif bold italic (𝙏𝙚𝙖𝙨𝙚𝙧 𝙗𝙚𝙡𝙤𝙬 👀 …) as in the Studio post. Use the styled letters on this line only, since screen readers stumble over them. You can add an optional open question for the reader: "I’d love to hear how you’re tackling similar problems in your own systems."

Then come the **hashtags**: three to five, lowercase and run together. Mix the broad topic tags (`#generativeui #genui #uxforagents #designengineering`) with one playful positioning tag that sums up the project (`#lovableformarketing`).

The **first comment** carries the links, not the post body. Links in the body tend to get less reach, and the post reads cleaner without them. Use one short label per link, and put the story first when it's the lighter read:

- `📖 The project story, my side of it: https://mariappan.netlify.app/work/<slug>/`
- `The engineering deep dive: https://mariappan.netlify.app/writing/<slug>/`, or simply `Blog:` and `Short Story:`

## Voice: what carries over from the site and what changes

Carries over from `share-my-work/references/voice.md`:

- Write in the first person, warm and plain. Put the team first, and use "I" for my own feelings and calls. Credit people by role ("our CTO"), never by name, unless the user says to.
- Never use hype words (leverage, seamless, game-changer, revolutionary) or brag lines ("only engineer", "no designer"). Never add metric flexes.
- Don't add em dashes, since they read as AI. If the user's draft has one, keep it or offer " - " or a full stop as an option. It's their call.
- Use the official capitalisation: Strive AI, Claude, GenUI. Use curly apostrophes (’), as in both posts.
- Never invent facts. Dates, the series link and quotes come from the published pieces or the user.

Differs from the site:

- **Emoji are welcome**, mostly at the ends of lines. Keep the user's own, from the wide set in the benchmarks. Don't add more than they would, and don't use emoji as bullets.
- **No headings or lists**, only short paragraphs. Unicode bold (sans-serif bold, or the bold italic on the teaser line) is fine for one or two short phrases, such as a name or a call to action.
- **No lede or `mark`, no "deep dive · ~12 min" labels**, and no section structure.
- **Don't summarise the project.** The architecture, the lessons and the outcome stay in the blog. One idea in the post is enough, and the reel carries the rest.
